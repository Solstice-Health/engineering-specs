
These experiments involve adding two different types of examples within the banner generation workspace: generic examples and golden branded examples.

**Test set:** Novocure, Lisraya, Ibsrela. 2 banners each. 4 dimensions per banner.

## 1. Generic examples

5 hand-built anonymized complete banners any brand can reference. Layout, motion, chrome. Brand stripped. Examples of potential anonymized banner patterns:
- Image to complementary image transition (i.e. vyndamax example where we transition from image of dad with daughter to similar image but the dad hugging the daughter closer)
- Zooming around the background image then blurring the background (i.e. the Vyndamax banner we made where the background moves to different positions when the text changes and then on the final frame the background blurs)
- Zooming into the subject of the banner for the CTA scene (i.e. NMOSD banner with the woman on stage - basically we visualize the subject from far away, and then zoom into the critical area when the CTA appears)

Run the 6 test banners with these 5 in context vs without. Modular brand units (animation / HTML / CSS) stay as they are for both tests.

## 2. Golden examples

1-3 high quality examples that each respective brand likes.

Run the same 6 test banners with goldens + style guide in context vs without.

## Acceptance

Same volume on every arm. Run all 6 banners × 4 sizes through control, generic, and golden.

Score all 4 sizes on all 6 banners.

- Generic: keep if we'd pick it over control on at least 16 of 24.
- Golden: case by case. Keep for a brand if it helps that brand and the output isn't a copy of the golden.

## Done

6 total sets of banners:
- 2 without either experiment
- 2 with generic examples
- 2 with golden examples

Based on these examples we will understand whether or not it makes sense to move forward with the generic example approach. We will also better understand the impact of golden examples which we may decide to use on a case by case basis.
